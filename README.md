# The Night Post

The mail doesn't stop at midnight. It just changes hands.

There's an office at the end of a street the daytime doesn't use. One
lamp, one counter, one bag that's never quite empty. You're the night
clerk now — nobody remembers hiring you, and the ledger doesn't care.
Every round a quota comes down from somewhere upstairs, and the mail in
your hand either meets it or the office closes with you inside.

The trick is the order. A wax seal doubles whatever you send next. An
express stamp hurries along everything after it. The night owl scores
for every piece already gone, so it goes last — and the courier brings
two more, so she goes first. Registered mail pays well and jams the bag
on its way through. Nine kinds of mail, three nights, and the whole job
is knowing what to send *when*.

Between rounds the shop shelf swings open. Coins buy the bag you'll
draw from tomorrow. Spend like the night depends on it.

**To play:** open it in your browser —
[clock in here](https://ordodox.github.io/nightpost/) — or grab
`index.html` and open it straight from disk. No install. Works with the
internet off, which suits the hours.

Your shift survives a closed tab. And if you want a friend to suffer
the exact same night — same mail, same shelf, same luck — the
*challenge link* button deals them your seed.

There's also **the midnight shift**: one shared shift a day, same mail
for everyone who clocks in. The *shift №* button (or a `?shift` link)
deals it. The group chat is the leaderboard.

The office keeps **a records shelf** — your best nights, by postage
delivered, on your own device and nowhere else. A night worked on a
bent table says so, right on the record. The shelf remembers even when
you'd rather it didn't.

And if the office's numbers don't suit you, post **standing orders**:
every quota and price is yours to bend, a night dealt under bent
numbers wears them openly, and a challenge link carries them — so a
friend suffers your custom night exactly, house rules and all.

Three nights. The quota always rises. The last bag is the heaviest.

Clock in. The mail's already waiting.
